## army
1930 · Germany
Enigma I enters service
The German army adopts a machine with three moving rotors, a plugboard and a fixed reflector.

## french
1931 · France
Documents change the picture
French intelligence obtains German manuals from Hans-Thilo Schmidt and passes intelligence material to the Poles.

## polish
1932–1939 · Poland
The Cipher Bureau
Marian Rejewski reconstructs the wiring. With Jerzy Różycki and Henryk Zygalski, he develops methods to read the traffic. In July 1939, Poland shares its work with France and Britain at Pyry.

## british
1940 · United Kingdom
Bletchley Park
British teams build on the Polish work. The British Bombe, designed by Alan Turing and improved by Gordon Welchman, speeds up the search for machine settings.

## atlantic
1942 · Atlantic
A fourth rotor
The introduction of M4 in parts of the German U-boat fleet temporarily interrupts Allied reading of that naval traffic.

## norway
After 1945 · Norway
A second life
Norway’s security service reuses captured Enigma I machines and rewires their rotors and reflector.
